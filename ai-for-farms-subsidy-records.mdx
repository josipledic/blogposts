It's a late June evening, and a farmer in Lower Saxony sits at the kitchen table with a pile of delivery slips and a phone full of photos of the sprayer display. Three weeks ago the winter wheat got a fungicide. Which field, which product, how many litres per hectare, at what growth stage? The rules say that record should have been written without delay. Instead it is being rebuilt from memory and a crumpled slip.

Repeated a hundred times a year, that scene is where I'd put AI on a farm. Not in the subsidy application, which is a declaration the farmer signs, but in capturing what happened in the field while it is fresh. This post answers, in question form, what the paperwork asks for as I checked it in late September 2026, and where a model helps and where it has to stay out.

I use Germany as the worked example, because its rules are well documented and several of them have just changed. The logic carries to other EU countries, and there is a short note on the US further down.

Read the Bavarian number with care. More than 20,000 farmers started that survey and 13,775 complete answers were evaluated, so the participants chose themselves. The 93% is the figure I keep in mind. The load is not only hours. It is the worry that one missing line costs money.

## When does the paperwork fall due?

<Timeline title="A German arable farm's paperwork year, as of late September 2026">
<Phase when="Before fertilising" title="Work out the need">The nitrogen and phosphate need of each field is calculated and recorded before anything is spread. Records and receipts are kept for seven years.</Phase>
<Phase when="After each pass" title="Fertiliser and spray records">Fertiliser applied: recorded within 14 days (it was two days until the end of 2024). Spray applications: recorded without delay, with the full content required since 1 January 2026.</Phase>
<Phase when="March to 15 May" title="Application season">State portals such as FIONA in Baden-Württemberg open in March. The CAP application is due on 15 May. Parcel changes and missing documents can follow without cuts until 31 May in that state.</Phase>
<Phase when="Mid-June to 30 September" title="Check the findings">Results of administrative checks and the area monitoring system appear in the portal. In Baden-Württemberg, certain findings could be corrected until 30 September 2026, unless an on-site check had been announced.</Phase>
<Phase when="30 June" title="State fertiliser reports">Some states want an annual report. Lower Saxony asked for the 2025 fertiliser year through its ENNI portal by 30 June 2026.</Phase>
<Phase when="1 January 2027" title="Electronic spray records">Machine-readable electronic records become mandatory in Germany. Until then, handwritten or electronic records are accepted if complete.</Phase>

The dates come from several state pages: the [Baden-Württemberg ministry](https://mlr.baden-wuerttemberg.de/de/unser-service/presse-und-oeffentlichkeitsarbeit/pressemitteilungen/pressemitteilung/pid/gemeinsamer-antrag-2026-ende-der-antragssaison-am-15-mai-2026) for the application season, Lower Saxony's [fertiliser authority](https://www.duengebehoerde-niedersachsen.de/duengebehoerde/news/35862_Aufzeichnungs-_und_Meldepflichten_gem_DueV_und_Landesduengeverordnung) and [plant protection service](https://www.pflanzenschutzdienst-niedersachsen.de/pflanzenschutz/news/29578_Dokumentationspflicht_von_Pflanzenschutzmittel-Anwendungen_Neue_Vorgaben_ab_2026) for the records, and Schleswig-Holstein's [chamber](https://www.lksh.de/aktuelles/nachrichten/news/artikel/show/wichtige-aenderung-bei-der-duengeverordnung-verlaengerte-frist-fuer-aufzeichnungen-ab-2025) for the 14-day rule. States differ in details such as portals and correction windows, so check your own Landwirtschaftskammer or agriculture office before you plan around a date.

## What does the CAP application ask for, and what changed in 2026?

The same form goes by different names depending on the state: Gemeinsamer Antrag, Mehrfachantrag, Sammelantrag or Agrarantrag. The [last day without late-filing reductions is 15 May](https://www.topagrar.com/betriebsleitung/news/diese-fristen-sind-fur-landwirte-im-mai-2026-wichtig-a-20025065.html), and the application is filed online in a state portal such as ANDI, ELAN, iBALIS or FIONA. In Baden-Württemberg, filing from 16 to 31 May means reductions, and after 31 May the application is rejected as late.

Two layers of change apply to 2026. In Germany, the amended rules on conditionality (the GLÖZ standards) and on eco-schemes were published at the end of 2025 and [apply since 1 January 2026](https://www.lwk-niedersachsen.de/lwk/news/43623_GAP_Was_aendert_sich_2026), including new conditions for the replacement grassland that has to be created when permanent grassland is converted. And at EU level, the simplification package became [Regulation (EU) 2025/2649](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2025/2649/oj/eng), published on 31 December 2025 and in force from 1 January 2026.

According to [press coverage of the agreement](https://www.eunews.it/en/2025/11/11/fewer-controls-less-red-tape-cap-simplification-approved/), it lifts the annual payment for small farmers to up to €3,000, introduces a "once-only" principle of at most one official on-the-spot check per farm and year, and was expected to save farmers up to €1.6 billion a year. Most of that relief works on rules and controls. The record-keeping in the next section did not get lighter. The spray records got stricter.

The ground is still moving. The [next CAP reform from 2028](https://www.lwk-niedersachsen.de/lwk/news/43623_GAP_Was_aendert_sich_2026) is in planning, so don't build anything that only makes sense for one year's form.

## Which records pile up between applications?

| Record | What the rule asks (Germany, September 2026) | Keep for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fertiliser need | Calculated and recorded per field before fertilising | 7 years |
| Fertiliser applied | Type and amount of nitrogen and phosphate per plot, within 14 days of each application | 7 years |
| Spray applications | Each one recorded without delay: use, who applied, date, rate actually applied, plot name and georeference (FLIK number or GPS point), crop with EPPO code and growth stage, among others | 3 years |
| Nutrient flow balance (Stoffstrombilanz) | Repealed on 7 July 2025, though some funding programmes may still ask for one | n/a |
| Work done by contractors | The contractor keeps complete records and passes them on without delay; you check them and secure the duty in the contract | as above |

Spray records carry the sharpest edge. Lower Saxony's plant protection service says that from 2026 the current year's records must be complete and are checked, and that missing, incomplete or late records already count as a conditionality breach this year, which puts direct payments at risk. From 1 January 2027, the electronic, machine-readable form is mandatory in Germany, at the latest 30 days after the application. A PDF does not count. A digital field record, or a structured file in CSV, JSON or XML, does. Lower Saxony's service adds that German law is being adapted to the EU rules, so expect details to shift.

Finding any record in seconds is the habit behind [audit day at a small food producer](/posts/ai-for-food-producers-compliance), and a control visit on a farm asks for the same thing, usually on a day nobody planned.

## Where does AI help, and who decides?

The job I'd give a model is narrow: turn a 20-second voice note or a photographed delivery slip into a draft record with every required field, and shout about the empty ones.

- **At the sprayer:** the operator dictates into a phone: "Fungicide on Am Mühlenweg, one litre per hectare, eight point four hectares, growth stage 39."
- **Draft:** the model transcribes and fills fixed fields. Plot names come from the farm's own field list, and crop and EPPO code come from that list too, never from the model's memory.
- **Checks in code:** the product must exist in the [BVL's online database of authorised products](https://www.bvl.bund.de/DE/Arbeitsbereiche/04_Pflanzenschutzmittel/01_Aufgaben/02_ZulassungPSM/01_ZugelPSM/01_OnlineDatenbank/psm_onlineDB_node.html), the dose is compared with the label, and mandatory fields must be present. I build software for a living, and rules like these belong in code, not in a prompt.
- **Farmer confirms:** one tap to confirm or edit. Only then does the record enter the field record. The model never confirms its own work.

Here is what the draft might look like. The data is invented.

```text
SPRAY RECORD, DRAFT, NOT CONFIRMED
Date: 14 May, 06:40 to 08:15. Applied by: operator on duty
Plot: Am Mühlenweg (matched to field list, FLIK from list)
Crop: winter wheat (EPPO code from list), growth stage 39
Product: "Example Fungizid" (found in authorised list)
Rate: 1.0 l/ha as spoken. Label maximum: check pending
Area: 8.4 ha
MISSING: reason for use not stated
```

Speech recognition copes with numbers well and with product names less well, because brand names often sound alike. That is why the product is matched against a list and a near miss is shown to the farmer instead of being corrected silently. Test it on your own recordings, with engine noise and dialect, before you trust it.

Everything in that sequence is drafting and checking. Everything below stays with the farmer, because it is where an error costs money and the signature is yours:

- Filling in or submitting the CAP application.
- Parcel numbers, areas and crop codes in the application.
- Choosing a product or a dose.
- Deciding whether a field meets a conditionality standard.

Other things fit AI, always with a check: reading delivery slips and invoices for product and quantity, drafting a message to a landlord or advisor about a parcel, and listing which records are still missing before an inspection. A reminder for 15 May is not on that list. A calendar entry does that better.

<Callout title="Never let a model guess parcel data" tone="warning">
You sign the application, so the responsibility for every area and crop in it is yours. A model that can't find a field number will sometimes produce a plausible one. Parcel identifiers, areas and boundaries come from the portal and your own field list. Anything the model can't match goes back to you as a gap, never as a guess. The same goes for product names and doses in spray records.
</Callout>

On those assumptions the tool barely pays for itself in time. Running costs are a few cents per record in model fees plus hosting, and a few hours of setup if it sits on an existing record sheet (my estimate, not a quote). The case for it is accuracy and nerves: no more rebuilding June from memory, and a gap in a record flagged the same day.

Often you don't need a model at all. Lower Saxony's service names [näon and PS Info MeinBetrieb](https://www.pflanzenschutzdienst-niedersachsen.de/pflanzenschutz/news/29578_Dokumentationspflicht_von_Pflanzenschutzmittel-Anwendungen_Neue_Vorgaben_ab_2026) for farms without a digital field record, and the Julius Kühn Institute offers a free web app called DiPAgE that needs no registration. For 2026 an Excel sheet is still accepted if it holds the required data. For the application itself, a chamber advisory service, a Beratungsring or your tax and farm advisor does what a chatbot can't: they know your state's portal and can be asked in person. The cheapest fix of all is a fixed template and one rule: no leaving the field without the record.

If you also sell from a farm shop, stock is a separate problem, covered in [farm shop stock planning](/posts/ai-for-farm-shops-inventory).

## What about data protection?

Farm records look harmless, but they hold names, addresses, bank details in applications, parcel locations tied to a person, and the voices of employees and contractors. A provider that processes your notes needs a data processing agreement under [Article 28 of the GDPR](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-28-gdpr/). I'd pick EU hosting, delete the audio once the transcript is confirmed, tell staff and contractors that their voice notes are processed, and keep application PDFs and lease contracts out of free consumer chatbots.

## And outside the EU?

The shape is the same elsewhere. In the US, [USDA's Farm Service Agency](https://www.fsa.usda.gov/news-events/news/07-01-2026/usda-reminds-ohio-producers-file-crop-acreage-reports) set 15 July 2026 as the acreage reporting date for corn, soybeans and most other crops in Ohio, with dates that vary by crop and county. The report needs the crop, its use, acres, a map with approximate boundaries, planting dates, producer shares and irrigation practice. The farmers.gov portal lets producers export field boundaries as shapefiles. Same rule as in Germany: a model can draft from field notes, but the acreage you report is yours.

## Questions farmers ask

### Can an AI assistant fill in my CAP application for me?

Not the application itself. It is filed through your state's portal, and the areas, crops and parcel numbers in it come from official data and your own field list. A model can help around it: listing which leases and documents are missing, drafting a note to a landlord, summarising the findings the portal shows after mid-June. It never submits and never invents a value.

### Do I have to record spray applications electronically this year?

You have to record every application completely since 1 January 2026, by hand or electronically. The machine-readable electronic form is mandatory in Germany from 1 January 2027, after the EU allowed member states to extend the start by a year. Other countries may have chosen differently.

### What happens if I file the CAP application late?

Late filing means reductions, and the application is rejected after a cut-off date. In Baden-Württemberg that date was 31 May 2026. Other states may differ slightly, so don't work from my example. The cheap protection is to file well before 15 May.

### How long do I have to keep the records?

Fertiliser records and receipts for seven years. Plant protection records for at least three years, counted from the start of the year after they arose. A funding programme you use may set its own period, so read its terms.

## Where I'd start

Record at the moment of work, let code check the entry against lists, and keep the declaration in your own hands. I'd start with the spray records, because the January 2027 rule makes them the first thing that has to change, and a field record app or the free JKI tool may be all you need. Add voice capture only if the evenings are what hurts.
