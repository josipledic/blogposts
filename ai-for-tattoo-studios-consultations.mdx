Picture a tattoo artist's phone on a Monday morning. Eleven new Instagram messages arrived overnight. Three say, in full: “how much for a small one?” One is a screenshot of someone else's tattoo and nothing more. One asks about Saturday. And one, after the third round of questions, turns out to come from a 17-year-old.

None of this is rude or unusual. A tattoo is a custom job priced by size, placement, detail and time, and nobody knows that before they ask. So the person who earns money with the needle spends part of the day running an interview by text message, one question per hour, across Instagram, WhatsApp and email.

That interview is a good job for an AI assistant, with one hard limit: it asks and explains, and the artist decides.

## One DM thread, taken apart

Here is what those messages are really asking, and what the artist has to find out before the conversation can go anywhere.

| What the customer writes | What the artist needs to know | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “How much for a small one?” | Size in cm, placement, black or colour, detail | Price and session length follow from these |
| “Something like this” plus a screenshot | Whose work it is, what should stay, how much room the artist gets | Whether the artist takes the job often depends on it |
| “On my arm” | Which part, a photo of the area, existing tattoos or scars | Placement changes technique and healing; scars and cover-ups need a different talk |
| “My budget is about 100” | Studio minimum, hourly rate, whether the idea fits | Better learned in message two than in message nine |
| “Saturday?” | Which artist, how long the session runs, whose books are open | A four-hour piece doesn't fit a two-hour gap |
| “I'm 17, my mum is fine with it” | Studio age policy, ID at the appointment | Law and policy differ by country |

One question at a time, that's a dozen messages. Some of those threads will end before anything is booked, and the artist can't tell which until the end.

## Start with a form and a link in your bio

The simplest fix isn't AI. A short request form with fields for idea, size, placement, references and availability, linked from your Instagram bio and sent as the first reply to every DM, takes most of the interview out of the chat. tatme describes exactly this setup, and TattooGenda advertises deposits and automatic reminders. Those are vendor descriptions, so check what each tool does and how it handles data. Instagram also has built-in [frequently asked questions](https://help.instagram.com/4153704678000867/) that appear when someone starts a message.

For a solo artist with five inquiries a week, that may be all you need.

AI earns its place when people ignore the form and keep sending DMs, which they will. Someone writes “something small on my wrist, like my friend's, how much?” at 11 p.m. The assistant turns that into the same fields, asks only for what's missing, and answers the standard questions that otherwise cost ten minutes each.

Three design choices do most of the work.

**It never invents a price.** The price depends on what the artist sees in the reference and on the skin. The assistant quotes only what you've published and says who gives the real number.

**Anything unusual goes up unchanged.** Cover-ups, scars, hands, neck or face, memorial pieces, anyone who sounds under 18: the card says so in its first line.

**One question at a time.** A wall of seven questions gets one-word answers or silence.

## What lands in the artist's inbox

The artist should be able to read the card in half a minute. Something like this:

```text
NEW INQUIRY, Tue 09:12 (Instagram)
Placement: inner forearm, left, about 10 cm tall
Idea: botanical sprig, fine line, black only. 3 references (link)
First tattoo: yes. Cover-up: no.
Budget: "around 150". Studio minimum 80, estimate is the artist's call.
Availability: Saturdays after 14:00, flexible in November
Age: says 18 or over, ID checked at the studio
Flag: reference 2 looks like another artist's piece. Ask what they like about it.
Suggested reply: yes to a consult, deposit terms attached.
```

Compare that with scrolling a four-day thread. The flag line matters most: the assistant noticed something the artist would want to raise, and left the raising to the artist.

## Deposits, and who decides on exceptions

Studios ask for a deposit because a no-show leaves a hole nobody can refill. The assistant adds consistency: it states your terms exactly, every time, before anyone pays. It never decides an exception such as illness or a family emergency. A cancelled slot is also easier to refill with a short waiting list, the same idea as [filling last-minute gaps in a salon book](/posts/ai-for-hair-salons-scheduling).

Deposit law is consumer-law territory and differs by country. A piece on tattoo law by the German firm [KKP.LAW](https://www.kleymann.com/aktuelles/news/tattoorecht-anzahlung-oder-terminkaution/) draws a distinction worth knowing. An Anzahlung (down payment) is part of the price, so it can't simply be forfeited if the contract ends. A Terminkaution (appointment security) only secures the appointment, and the studio can agree that it is forfeited if the customer doesn't show. The firm also says the amount should be agreed individually rather than put into general terms and conditions, where such clauses are mostly seen as ineffective. That's one law firm's view, not a court ruling, so show your wording to a lawyer before the assistant sends it.

## Age: the assistant asks, the door checks

In the UK, tattooing someone under 18 is an offence under the [Tattooing of Minors Act 1969](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1969/24), although it is a defence that the artist had reasonable cause to believe the person was 18 or over and did believe it. Germany has no statutory minimum age. According to the [Federal Ministry of Justice's youth information site](https://www.recht-relaxed.de/WebS/RechtRelaxed/DE/KoerperSex/TattoosPiercings/tattoosPiercings_node.html), a tattoo counts as bodily harm unless the person consents, whether a teenager can consent depends on maturity, and in practice studios tattoo from 18, or from 16 with written parental consent.

So the studio sets the policy and the assistant applies it. It asks “are you 18 or older?”, records the answer, and passes anything else to the artist. A chat can't verify age. A person with an ID card at the door can.

## The ink question

Customers also ask about the EU ink rules, because they've heard colours were banned. According to the [European Commission](https://cyprus.representation.ec.europa.eu/news/chemicals-new-eu-rules-safer-ink-tattoos-across-eu-2022-01-04_en), a restriction on hazardous substances in tattoo inks applied from 4 January 2022, with a ban on Pigment Blue 15 and Pigment Green 7 following on 4 January 2023.

Germany's Federal Institute for Risk Assessment adds caution in its [tattoo ink FAQ](https://www.bfr.bund.de/en/service/frequently-asked-questions/topic/frequently-asked-questions-about-tattoo-inks/): inks have no approval procedure, the manufacturer is responsible for safety, no list of safe colours exists, and the vast majority of complications are local skin irritation or allergic reactions. It also points to the European standard EN 17169 on safe and hygienic practice, which isn't legally binding.

So the assistant shouldn't say “our inks are safe.” It should say what you know: which brands you use, what your supplier confirms about the restriction, whether you follow EN 17169. Everything else goes to you.

## Data protection and platform rules

**Say it's a bot.** According to the European Commission, [Article 50 of the AI Act](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/transparency-obligations-under-article-50-ai-act) has applied since 2 August 2026, so people must be told when they're talking to an AI system.

**Keep health questions out of the DMs.** Allergies, skin conditions, medication and pregnancy are data concerning health, which [Article 9 of the GDPR](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-9-gdpr/) protects strictly. I'd leave those questions to the consent form at the studio, as covered in the post on [beauty salon consultation records](/posts/ai-for-beauty-salons-consultation-records), and configure the assistant not to ask. If a customer volunteers something, it passes it to the artist and doesn't file the details.

**Know who processes what.** Reference images and placement photos are personal data. Any AI or form provider that sees them is your processor, and [Article 28](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-28-gdpr/) requires a contract. Ask where data is processed and whether it trains models, and set a deletion period for unanswered and declined inquiries.

**WhatsApp has its own terms.** Since 15 January 2026, Meta's terms bar providers of general-purpose AI assistants from the WhatsApp Business platform. [TechCrunch reports](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/18/whatssapp-changes-its-terms-to-bar-general-purpose-chatbots-from-its-platform) that Meta said businesses using AI to serve their own customers aren't affected. Check your provider's setup first.

## Where AI is the wrong tool

**The design.** Customers will bring AI-generated images as references, and that's fine. Whether a design works on skin and whether the artist will do it is the artist's call, and the drawing is what the customer pays for. The assistant never creates or edits designs.

**The price, the no, and the delicate yes.** The assistant reads out what you've published. Cover-ups, someone else's tattoo to rework, a piece for a person who has died: these conversations need a person.

**A small studio.** One artist with a link in the bio, a form and a few saved replies doesn't need a model.

## Questions artists and studio owners ask

### Can an AI chatbot quote a tattoo price?

Only the numbers you've published, such as a minimum price or an hourly rate. A real quote depends on the reference and the placement, so the assistant should say who will give it and when.

### Will customers mind talking to a bot?

Some will, and they can use the form or wait for the artist. Most people sending a DM at 11 p.m. want to know what happens next. A short, clear message asking about placement and size does that better than silence.

### Can it collect the deposit?

It can send the terms and a payment link. The money should run through your payment provider or booking tool, and holding a slot stays the artist's decision. Several tattoo booking tools advertise deposit links, so check yours first.

## Where I'd start

Write the answers sheet first, because it's useful even if you never build anything. Put a form in your bio. Then let an assistant take only the first reply and the missing details for a month, and fix the sheet whenever the artist corrects a card.

The rule of thumb: make the first reply instant and keep the final yes personal.
