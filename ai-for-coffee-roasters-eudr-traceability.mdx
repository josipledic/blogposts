If you last read about the EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR) in 2025, three things you remember are probably out of date. The start date moved to 30 December 2026. Companies further down the supply chain no longer file their own due diligence statements. And a roaster is no longer classed as a mere trader: the Commission corrected its own FAQ on exactly that point on 21 August 2026.

The regulation was adopted in 2023, postponed in December 2024, postponed and simplified again in December 2025, and clarified several times since. This post gives the state as I checked it in late September 2026, in question-and-answer form, and then looks at where AI helps a small roastery with the paperwork. Less than the sales pitches suggest, but in one place it is worth having.

First, some context. The German Coffee Association [estimates about 900 companies](https://www.kaffeeverband.de/de/faq/) in Germany roast coffee for a living (not just now and then for marketing), around 850 of them smaller roasters. Very few of those import their own containers. Most buy green coffee from an importer, and that matters, because the rules for "buys from an importer" are far lighter than the rules for "imports".

## Is coffee in scope, and when do the rules start?

Yes. Annex I of the regulation lists heading 0901: coffee, whether or not roasted or decaffeinated, plus husks, skins and coffee substitutes containing coffee. Green and roasted beans are both covered. Soluble coffee (extracts, essences and concentrates, code 2101 11 00) is being added by [Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/2102](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_del/2026/2102/oj), published on 17 September 2026, and applies from 30 December 2027.

The 2026 and 2027 dates come from the [Commission's EUDR page](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/topics/forests/deforestation/regulation-deforestation-free-products_en), its [trade service summary](https://trade.ec.europa.eu/access-to-markets/en/news/delay-until-december-2026-and-other-developments-implementation-eudr-regulation) and the amending [Regulation (EU) 2025/2650](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2025/2650/oj).

Will 30 December 2026 hold? The Commission's [May 2026 press release](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/ip_26_941) says it is focused on a successful entry into application on that date, and the review package held a report, updated guidance and FAQ, and a delegated act on product scope, but no proposal to amend the regulation itself. That is the best evidence there is. I still wouldn't bet the roastery on it, because the date has moved twice, each time in the last weeks before it applied. Even official pages lag: when I checked, the [German customs page](https://www.zoll.de/DE/Fachthemen/Verbote-Beschraenkungen/Schutz-der-Pflanzenwelt/Entwaldungsfreie-Produkte/entwaldungsfreie-produkte_node.html) still showed 30 December 2025. Prepare the parts that are cheap and useful whatever the date, and don't pay for anything that only makes sense if it holds.

## Which role am I: operator, downstream operator or trader?

The regulation sorts companies by what they do with a specific product, not by what they call themselves. According to the [European Coffee Federation's summary](https://www.ecf-coffee.org/european-commission-clarifies-eudr-roles-across-supply-chains-including-coffee/) of the 21 August corrigendum, any change in the product's customs code makes the company that places the derived product on the market a downstream operator. Roasting green coffee (CN 0901 11 00) into roasted coffee (0901 21 00) is such a change.

| Your situation | Your role | What the regulation asks | Applies from |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| You bring green coffee into the EU yourself, or you are the first EU company to take it from a non-EU seller | Operator | Full due diligence: plot geolocation, risk assessment, a due diligence statement in the EU Information System, records for five years | 30 Dec 2026, or 30 Jun 2027 if you were a micro or small company by 31 Dec 2024 |
| You buy green coffee from an EU importer and roast it | Downstream operator | Keep supplier and customer details, plus the importer's reference numbers, for five years. No statement. No registration if you are an SME | 30 Dec 2026 |
| You import some lots, buy others, then roast | Both, lot by lot | Operator duties for what you import, downstream duties for what you buy | Per role |
| You resell roasted coffee you did not roast | Trader | Supplier and customer details for five years; reference numbers only if your supplier is an operator | 30 Dec 2026 |
| You sell soluble coffee | Not in scope yet | Nothing until the new scope applies | 30 Dec 2027 |

Until that corrigendum, the Commission's own example treated a roaster as a trader. For a small roastery the practical change is nil, because downstream operators and traders have identical obligations under Article 5 of the [consolidated regulation](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:02023R1115-20251226).

Size matters in two ways. SMEs in the downstream roles do not register in the Information System; only larger companies do. And micro or small importers that existed by the end of 2024 get until 30 June 2027. The [European Coffee Federation](https://www.ecf-coffee.org/european-union-deforestation-regulation-eudr/) lists the thresholds: a company is micro if it stays under at least two of 450,000 euros balance sheet, 900,000 euros net turnover and 10 employees, and small if it stays under at least two of 5 million euros, 10 million euros and 50 employees.

One wrinkle on dates. The June 2027 extension in Article 38(3) is written for "operators", and downstream operators are defined separately. The Commission's trade service lists downstream operators and traders of all sizes under 30 December 2026. So a roaster that only buys from importers should plan for December 2026. If your role is unclear, ask your national authority; in Germany that is the BLE.

## What do I actually have to keep?

As a downstream operator or trader, Article 5 asks for four things:

- **Supplier details:** name, trade name, postal and email address, and web address if there is one.
- **Reference numbers:** the due diligence statement reference numbers or declaration identifiers, but only when your direct supplier is the operator (Germany's BLE calls this the +1 rule). Buy through a merchant who is not the importer and the text asks only for the merchant's details.
- **Customer details:** the same data for the downstream operators or traders you supply, such as cafés and shops that resell your coffee. A private customer buying a bag is neither.
- **Five years:** keep it all for five years and give it to the authority on request.

There is also a hard stop. You may place coffee on the market only if you hold this information. And if you learn something that suggests a lot is non-compliant, Article 5 tells you to inform the authority and your customers immediately.

Most of this is on your invoices already. Food law has long required you to identify your immediate supplier and customers, the "one step back, one step forward" principle in [Article 18 of the General Food Law](https://www.fsai.ie/enforcement-and-legislation/legislation/food-legislation/general-principles-of-food-law). What is new is the reference number and the five-year clock.

If you are the importer, it is a different job. Article 9 asks for the geolocation of every plot (latitude and longitude with six decimal digits, polygons for plots over four hectares), the production period, and verifiable evidence that the coffee is deforestation-free and legally produced. Land cleared after 31 December 2020 disqualifies the lot. The maximum fine must be at least 4% of your Union-wide annual turnover under Article 25.

## Where does AI help, and who decides?

For a roaster who buys from importers, AI is useful for one thing: turning the documents that arrive into a clean lot register, and noticing what is missing before the coffee goes into the roaster.

<Step title="Green coffee arrives" who="Importer, warehouse" time="delivery day">Invoice, delivery note and the importer's reference number come as PDFs, scans or an email.</Step>
<Step title="Extract and check completeness" who="AI" tone="ai" time="minutes">Reads the documents, fills the register: supplier details, invoice and lot number, quantity, origin, reference number. Flags every empty field.</Step>
<Step title="Release or hold" who="Roaster or buyer" tone="human" time="2 min per lot">Compares the record with the document and decides. Missing reference number means hold.</Step>
<Step title="Chase the gap" who="AI, then person" tone="ai">The model drafts a short request to the importer. A person reads it and sends it.</Step>
<Step title="Link to roast batches" who="System">Roast log batch IDs (from Cropster, Artisan or a sheet) are tied to green lots, so you can say which roasts contained lot X.</Step>
<Step title="Answer a request" who="Roaster" tone="human" time="on demand">If the authority or a customer asks, the register produces suppliers, reference numbers and customers for the lot. A person checks it before it goes out.</Step>
</Flow>

Here is what a record might look like after step two. The data is invented.

```text
LOT 2026-041, green, Ethiopia, 20 sacks of 60 kg
Supplier: Example Coffee Imports GmbH, Hamburg, buying@example-coffee.example
Invoice: R-2026-1187
Reference number: MISSING (not on invoice or delivery note)
Status: HOLD. Request drafted for buyer to send.
```

The gate in step three is the point of the design. The model releases nothing. It makes a missing number impossible to overlook, which is what a busy week breaks.

## What can AI not do here?

<Callout title="A model cannot tell you a lot is compliant" tone="warning">
Whether coffee is deforestation-free and legally produced is established by the operator who places it on the market, with plot data, satellite checks and a risk assessment. Nothing a roaster extracts from an invoice proves it. Never let a model guess a missing reference number, fill a gap with a plausible one, or write "compliant" in the register. A certificate on the delivery note is also not a reference number. If something looks wrong, the decision to tell the authority belongs to you.
</Callout>

If you do import directly, the same logic applies with more at stake. A language model can read a messy plot list from an exporter, but whether coordinates have six decimals, whether a polygon is needed, and whether a point lies in the right country are rule checks. Those belong in plain code or a specialist geodata tool. I have spent eight years building production software, and the rule I trust most is that validation goes in code, not in a prompt.

## What about data protection and enforcement?

Supplier and customer contact data is mostly business data. But a direct-trade lot may come with names of individual farmers next to the coordinates of their plots, and under [Article 4 of the GDPR](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-4-gdpr/) location data linked to an identifiable person is personal data. If those documents go to an AI provider, use one with an EU hosting option and a data processing agreement, and don't paste supplier contracts into free consumer tools.

On enforcement, Member States set the penalties. Germany's cabinet [adopted the national implementing bill](https://www.bundesregierung.de/breg-de/aktuelles/eu-entwaldungsverordnung-2449430) on 12 August 2026, naming the BLE as the responsible authority. The bill, the [Entwaldungs- und Waldschädigungs-Minimierungs-Gesetz](https://www.deutsche-handwerks-zeitung.de/entwaldungsgesetz-beschlossen-verbaende-hoffen-auf-korrekturen-im-bundestag-382646/), is now in the Bundestag. A July draft reportedly put [fines of up to 20,000 euros](https://www.forstpraxis.de/entwaldung-eudr-verstoesse-100000-eur-kosten-freiheitstrafen-drohen-24704) on failing to keep records for five years. Treat that figure as provisional until the law passes.

## Questions roasters ask

### Do I have to register in the EUDR Information System?

Only if you are an operator (an importer), or a downstream operator or trader that is not an SME. SME roasters buying from importers do not register and do not file statements. They collect and keep information.

### Do I need farm coordinates if I buy from an importer?

No. Geolocation is part of the operator's due diligence under Article 9. You need the importer's reference number, and you should be able to produce it for five years.

### What if my importer won't give me the reference number?

Then you can't lawfully place that coffee on the market, because you don't hold the information Article 5 requires. Put the requirement in your purchase terms, hold the lot, and treat a supplier that can't provide it as a risk.

### Will the deadline move again?

Nobody can promise either way. The Commission is working towards 30 December 2026 and its May review proposed no change to the regulation itself, but earlier dates moved at the last minute. Check the [Commission's EUDR page](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/topics/forests/deforestation/regulation-deforestation-free-products_en) before you spend money.

## Where I'd start

Treat the reference number like a food safety certificate: no number, no roast. This quarter, build the sheet, add the sentence to your purchase terms and ask every importer for their numbers. Look at AI only if the paperwork outgrows the sheet, and never delegate the question of whether a lot is compliant.
