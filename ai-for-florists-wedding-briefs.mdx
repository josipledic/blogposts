The consultation went well. The couple brought a Pinterest board with 60 pins, described their style as "boho but elegant, not too much greenery", fell in love with dusty pink, and asked for peonies. The wedding is on the second Saturday of September. The florist took notes on a pad, photographed a few pins, promised a proposal within the week, and then had three funerals, a corporate order and a Mother's Day hangover of invoices to deal with.

The proposal goes out twelve days later. The couple has meanwhile added two bridesmaids, changed the ceremony from the church to the garden, and asked a friend whether peonies are even available in September. (Mostly not, outside expensive imports.) The florist's notes don't mention the arch they discussed briefly at the end. On the wedding day, the venue coordinator points out that petals aren't allowed on the lawn.

None of these problems are about floristry skill. They're about turning a lively, visual, emotional conversation into a clear, written brief, quickly, and keeping it up to date as things change. That's the part AI can help with.

## Why the brief matters so much

Wedding flowers are a significant spend and a high-emotion one. The Knot's research puts the average US couple's spend on wedding flowers at [$2,800](https://www.theknot.com/content/average-cost-wedding-flowers), with a wide range from under $1,000 to nearly $5,000 for the middle half of couples, as part of an [average wedding budget of $34,000](https://www.theknotww.com/press-releases/the-knot-worldwide-unveils-2026-real-weddings-study). For a small florist, a wedding can be a week's revenue, and the difference between profit and loss often lies in the details agreed, or not agreed, at the consultation: quantities, substitutions, set-up times, what happens to the arch after the ceremony.

A clear brief protects both sides. The couple sees exactly what they're getting. The florist knows exactly what to order, make and deliver, and has a record when "we thought the arch was included" comes up.

## What the brief contains

A good brief reads like a production sheet with a heart. It captures what the couple wants in their words, and what the florist will actually make in numbers.

| Item | Quantity | Style and flowers | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bridal bouquet | 1 | Loose, garden style; dusty pink, cream, touch of burgundy; garden roses instead of peonies | Ribbon in cream silk; bride's grandmother's brooch to be attached |
| Bridesmaid bouquets | 4 (was 2) | Smaller versions of the bridal bouquet | Two added after the consultation |
| Buttonholes | 6 | Spray rose and eucalyptus | Groom's buttonhole slightly larger |
| Ceremony arch | 1 | Asymmetric, one side heavy; pampas and roses | Ceremony moved to the garden; arch to be moved to the dinner area after |
| Table centrepieces | 12 | Low arrangements, bud vases around | Tables of 8; no tall pieces blocking sight lines |
| Welcome sign flowers | 1 | Small swag | Venue sign, florist to confirm size |
| Petals | none | | Venue does not allow petals on the lawn |

**Venue and timing:** access from 11:00; ceremony 14:00; arch moved to the dinner area by 17:30; collection of vases and arch the next day before 12:00.

**Style words from the couple:** "boho but elegant", "not too much greenery", "soft, a bit wild", "nothing that looks like a hotel lobby".

That last line might be the most useful one in the brief. The couple's own words are how the florist checks later whether the design still feels like what they asked for.

## The peony problem

Couples ask for flowers they've seen online, often without knowing when they're in season or what they cost at other times of year. Peonies in September, ranunculus in August, dahlias in April. The model can check requested flowers against the florist's own seasonal list and flag the ones that will be expensive, unreliable or unavailable for the date, with suggested alternatives that give a similar look. The florist decides what to propose and how to explain it.

It's a small thing that changes the conversation. "Peonies won't be at their best in September, but garden roses in these shades give that same full, soft look, and they'll hold up better in the heat" sounds like expertise. Discovering the problem two weeks before the wedding sounds like a problem.

## Costing from recipes

Most florists price weddings by building recipes: how many stems of what go into each piece, what they cost, plus sundries, labour and delivery. It's accurate and slow. With a set of standard recipes and current wholesale prices, the model can draft the costing in minutes, and the florist adjusts.

Once recipes live in one place, changes become easy to price: "Two more bridesmaid bouquets" or "switch the arch to a heavier design" is recalculated in a moment, and the couple gets an updated proposal the same day instead of a vague "that'll be a bit more".

## What the couple receives

The proposal should arrive while the consultation is still fresh, ideally within two days. It opens with the couple's own words, so they recognise their wedding in it:

> You told us you wanted "boho but elegant, soft and a bit wild, nothing that looks like a hotel lobby". Here's how we'd bring that to your garden ceremony on 12 September: a loose bridal bouquet in dusty pink, cream and a touch of burgundy, with garden roses in place of peonies, which are rarely at their best in September. An asymmetric arch with pampas and roses, moved to the dinner area after the ceremony. Twelve low centrepieces so everyone can see each other across the table.

Then the item list, the prices, the timings, and a clear note of what's not included. The model drafts it from the brief; the florist adds the photos, adjusts the words and sends it.

## Keep a change log

Weddings change. Guest numbers move, bridesmaids are added, the ceremony moves indoors because of the weather forecast. Every change should be written down with its effect on price and logistics, and confirmed by the couple. The model can keep that log from emails and messages ("They've added two bridesmaids, can you update?"), recalculate the price, and draft the confirmation. The florist approves before it goes out.

The change log is what settles the question on the day: "we agreed on four bridesmaid bouquets on the 3rd of July, here's your confirmation."

## Tools that fit

Florist and event software such as Details Flowers Software, Curate or Stemcounter handles recipes, proposals and ordering, and some now add AI features. Many small florists work with spreadsheets, a shared folder of inspiration images and email. The AI layer can work with either: it reads the consultation transcript, notes and photos, drafts the brief and the costing from your recipes and price list, and keeps the change log. The design decisions, and the relationship with the couple, stay with you.

Consultation recordings and photos are personal data: ask for consent, keep them with the booking, and delete recordings once the brief is signed off.

## Questions florists ask

### Can AI design the arrangements?

It can suggest combinations and generate mood images, which some florists find useful for talking through ideas. It doesn't know how a flower behaves, how long it lasts out of water in August, or how to make an arch that survives a gust of wind. The design is yours.

### Will couples mind being recorded?

Most don't, especially when you explain it's so you can listen properly instead of writing everything down. Offer notes and a voice memo afterwards as the alternative.

### How accurate is the costing?

As accurate as your recipes and prices. Keep wholesale prices current, especially for seasonal and imported flowers, and treat the model's costing as a draft you check line by line.

### Does this help with smaller events too?

Yes. Corporate events, funerals and parties all benefit from a clear written brief. Funerals in particular, where families are under strain and details matter, deserve a brief that gets everything right the first time.

## Rule of thumb

Capture the couple's words, turn them into numbers the same day, and write down every change. Let the model do the transcribing, the season check and the arithmetic, and keep your own time for the flowers.
