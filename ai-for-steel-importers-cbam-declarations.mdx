AI can take most of the data chase out of a CBAM declaration. It reads your customs declarations for 2026, groups every import line of steel, aluminium, cement or fertiliser by CN code, country of origin, supplier and producing installation, and shows for each group whether you hold verified actual emissions or will have to use the Commission's default values. It drafts the requests to suppliers for what is missing, reads the operator emissions reports and verification reports that come back, checks them against your import volumes, and keeps a running estimate of how many certificates you will need. Your compliance staff still decide which values go into the declaration and submit it. What changes is that they spend the next ten months on the suppliers that matter, instead of on spreadsheets.

The deadline is fixed. Under [Article 6 of the CBAM Regulation, as amended by Regulation (EU) 2025/2083](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32025R2083), each authorised CBAM declarant submits a CBAM declaration for the preceding calendar year by 30 September, for the first time in 2027 for the year 2026, and surrenders the matching number of CBAM certificates by the same date. The declaration covers the total quantity of each type of goods, its embedded emissions, the certificates due after deductions for a carbon price paid abroad and for EU ETS free allocation, and the verification reports for any actual values used.

Metal traders and stockholders are only part of the picture. Fabricators, machine builders, fastener and tube distributors and anyone else who imports more than 50 tonnes of CBAM goods a year is in scope. But a metals wholesaler with eight-figure revenue, a dozen staff and a few hundred import lines a year from Turkey, India, China or Ukraine is the typical case, and one person usually owns customs compliance on top of other work.

The [50-tonne threshold](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32025R2083) in the new Article 2a counts the net mass of all CBAM goods across iron and steel, aluminium, fertilisers and cement together, per importer and calendar year. The recitals of the amending regulation say it was set so that at least 99 % of embedded emissions stay in scope. If you cross it, every tonne of the year counts, not only the tonnes above it.

## Default values or actual values

For each import, embedded emissions are either actual emissions reported by the producing installation and verified by an accredited verifier, or the [default values set by the Commission](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism/cbam-legislation-and-guidance_en) in Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2621. The Commission's [introductory guidance](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/document/download/031abf60-21a9-4822-b258-ac8b111d8358_en?filename=Guidance%20No.%201%20-%20Introduction%20to%20CBAM%20concepts.pdf) is plain about the choice: non-EU producers are not obliged to do anything, and if you do not receive verified actual data you can always use default values. Default values carry a mark-up, so a mill whose production is cleaner than the default will want its own verified data used, and so will you.

Verified data is the bottleneck. The Commission's [verification page](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism/cbam-verification_en) said the first verifiers were expected to be accredited around September 2026, and verification of a full year's emissions can only happen after that year ends. So the verification reports for 2026 production will arrive in 2027, mill by mill, between January and September.

## The numbers for one importer

The cost per tonne depends on the producer. The Commission's [guidance on the free allocation adjustment](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/document/download/3aa2c730-4f1b-4524-8f71-d9cfe4880ac7_en?filename=Guidance%20No.%204%20-%20CBAM%20calculation%20of%20the%20free%20allocation%20adjustment%20to%20the%20number%20of%20CBAM%20certificates%20to%20be%20surrendered.pdf) uses an example good with actual emissions of 1.5 tonnes of CO2 per tonne and a benchmark of 1.1. In 2026 the CBAM factor is 97.5 %, so free allocation covers 1.1 × 97.5 % of the benchmark and only the rest needs certificates. By 2030 the factor falls to 51.5 %, and to zero in 2034.

From 2027 this is also a cash question. Under the amended [Article 22(2)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32025R2083), at every quarter end you must hold certificates for at least 50 % of the embedded emissions of everything imported since 1 January, based on default values without the mark-up, or on last year's surrendered certificates for the same goods and origins, after the free allocation adjustment. A certificate not surrendered by 30 September costs the [EU ETS excess emissions penalty](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32025R2083) for each missing certificate, and the competent authority may reduce it only where wrong information came from an operator, verifier or certifier.

## Why the declaration is hard

**Customs data does not name the mill.** A customs line has a CN code, a country of origin, a supplier and a net mass. The CBAM declaration needs the producing installation. A trader in Istanbul may ship coil from three mills on one invoice.

**Suppliers answer in many formats.** Operator emissions reports arrive as the Commission's template, as a PDF, as a mill certificate with a number written on it, or as an email saying "we will send it after verification".

**Precursors change the number.** For complex goods such as tubes or fasteners, the embedded emissions of the steel used to make them count too, and actual values for a precursor only count if they come from a verification report covering the period when it was produced.

**Tonnages have to reconcile.** The emissions reported per installation must cover the tonnes you actually imported from it, by CN code. Gaps between the operator's report and your customs data are what a reviewer will find first.

**Three systems hold the answer.** Customs declarations sit with your broker or in the customs software, purchase orders and mill certificates in the ERP, and supplier correspondence in the purchasing team's mailboxes.

## What the assistant does

**Builds the import register.** It reads the 2026 customs declarations, by electronic export from your customs software or your broker, and lists every CBAM import line with CN code, origin, supplier, net mass and quarter, and it keeps the running total against the 50-tonne threshold.

**Maps lines to installations.** From purchase orders, mill certificates and delivery notes it proposes the producing installation for each line, with the document it relied on, and flags lines where the supplier is a trader and the mill is unknown.

**Chases the data.** For each installation without verified data it drafts the request to the supplier in English or the supplier's language, naming the goods, tonnages and period, and asking for the operator's emissions report and the verification report. It tracks replies and reminders.

**Checks what comes back.** It reads each operator report and verification report, checks that the installation, period, goods and CN codes match your imports, that the verifier is accredited, and that the emission values are plausible against the default value for the same goods and country. Mismatches go to a review list with the reason.

**Forecasts certificates.** For each quarter it estimates the certificates needed with actual values where they exist and default values where they do not, using the published quarterly prices, so finance can plan the 2027 purchases and the quarterly holding.

**Drafts the declaration file.** It prepares the totals per goods type, installation and country in the structure of the CBAM registry, for your compliance lead to review, correct and submit.

## Where it goes wrong

<Callout title="The assistant gathers and checks. It does not make the declaration." tone="warning">
The authorised CBAM declarant is responsible for the declaration, even when it delegates the submission. Treat the assistant's mapping and checks as a review list, keep every value traceable to a document, and have the person who signs off read the flagged lines. Nothing is submitted without that person.
</Callout>

**Every number needs its document.** The amended Article 7(5) requires records detailed enough for a verifier and the competent authority to check the calculation. An installation match without the order or certificate behind it is not a record.

**Supplier data is commercially sensitive.** Mills share emissions data reluctantly, and your purchase prices sit next to it. Run the assistant in your own environment or under contract terms you control, never in a consumer chatbot.

**A plausible number is not a verified one.** The assistant can say that a value looks low next to the default. It cannot verify it. Only an accredited verifier's report makes an actual value usable.

**Rules keep moving.** Default values were corrected in 2026, the Commission has proposed extending CBAM to downstream goods, and from 1 January 2027 [CBAM also applies in Norway and Iceland](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism/cbam-definitive-regime_en). Check the legislation page before each quarter's forecast.

## Questions about CBAM declarations

### When is the first CBAM declaration due?

By 30 September 2027, covering goods imported in 2026, under Article 6(1) of Regulation (EU) 2023/956 as amended by Regulation (EU) 2025/2083. The certificates for those emissions must be surrendered by the same date.

### Who has to submit a CBAM declaration?

Authorised CBAM declarants: importers, or indirect customs representatives acting for them, whose imports of iron and steel, aluminium, fertiliser and cement goods exceed 50 tonnes net mass in a calendar year. Electricity and hydrogen imports are not covered by that exemption.

### What happens if a supplier does not provide emissions data?

You use the default values set by the Commission for those goods and that country. They carry a mark-up, so they usually cost more than verified actual values from a cleaner producer.

### How much does a CBAM certificate cost?

For 2026 imports, the Commission publishes a quarterly price based on EU ETS auction prices: €75.36 for Q1, €75.28 for Q2 and €82.32 for Q3 2026. From 2027 the price is published weekly.

### What is the quarterly 50 % rule?

From 2027, at the end of each quarter, an authorised CBAM declarant must hold certificates for at least 50 % of the embedded emissions of the year's imports so far, under Article 22(2) of the amended regulation.
