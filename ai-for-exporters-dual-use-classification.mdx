AI can prepare the export control rating of every product you sell: read the datasheet, drawings and software notes, compare the performance figures with the control list entries that could apply, write down which parameters were checked against which threshold, and propose a rating or "not listed" for a person to confirm. When the list changes, it can show which products sit under an amended entry. When you export under a licence, it can keep the records the licence conditions ask for. Your export control manager still decides the rating and signs it. What changes is that the decision starts from a worked file, not a blank form.

This matters now because the UK is changing the rules in both directions at once. On 12 October 2026 the Export Control Joint Unit (ECJU) [paused its Control List Classification service](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/notice-to-exporters-202622-temporary-pause-of-the-control-list-classification-clc-service/notice-to-exporters-202622-temporary-pause-of-the-control-list-classification-clc-service) and asked exporters not to send new requests, because licence applications have become more complex. And the [Export Control (Amendment) Regulations 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/notice-to-exporters-202624-proposed-amendments-to-uk-strategic-export-controls/notice-to-exporters-202624-proposed-amendments-to-uk-strategic-export-controls) are due to bring new military and dual-use lists into force on 1 December 2026. You rate your own goods, against a list that is about to move.

The licence values are declared by exporters, and the ECJU warns that some are set high to avoid running out of licence, so treat the £1.4m as an order of magnitude, not an average sale. The order of magnitude is the point: the companies doing this work ship machine tools, sensors, lasers, test equipment, aerospace parts, encryption products and specialist materials, often in six- and seven-figure orders. The European Commission's [report on 2024 data](https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/ST-12011-2026-INIT/en/pdf) (COM(2026) 296) shows the same picture across the EU: €77.6 billion of authorised dual-use trade, 3% of all goods exported outside the EU. Individual export authorisations covered €24 billion of it, and member states issued 18,549 individual licences.

Every one of those changes lands on the same piece of work: the rating of each product. A wider open licence only helps if you know which entry your goods fall under. A list update only affects you if one of your products sits under an amended entry. Without classification advice from ECJU, the evidence for the rating has to be yours.

## Why the rating file is the slow part

**The information sits in five places.** Performance figures are in the datasheet, the real tolerances are in the drawings, the encryption functions are in a software release note, and the customer's intended use is in an email to sales. The control entries are written as technical thresholds with notes, decontrol notes and definitions in a separate section.

**Most products are "not listed", and nobody wrote down why.** A rating of "not listed" is still a decision. If HMRC or a customer's compliance team asks why a product needs no licence, "we checked once" is not an answer.

**Lists move every year.** The multilateral regimes agree changes, the EU and UK update their lists, and each update may touch a handful of your products or none. Finding out which means rereading every rating against the new text.

**Licences come with records.** [Article 29 of the Export Control Order 2008](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2008/3231/article/29) requires anyone using a general licence to keep registers with a description of the goods, dates, quantities, consignee and, where known, end user, for at least three years from the end of the calendar year (four for some licences). Airbus's settlement was mostly for failing to keep exactly those records.

## What the classification assistant does

**Reads the product evidence.** It pulls the specification figures from datasheets, drawings, test reports and software notes into one sheet per product, each figure with the page it came from.

**Proposes candidate entries.** It searches the consolidated list for entries whose wording and parameters could apply, and shows each threshold next to your figure: above, below, or not stated in your documents. Missing figures become questions for engineering, not guesses.

**Drafts the rating record.** For a person to confirm: the proposed rating or "not listed", the entries considered and why each was ruled in or out, the decontrol notes relied on, and the date and list version checked.

**Finds what a list amendment touches.** When new list text arrives, it compares old and new entries and returns the products whose ratings cite a changed entry, or whose figures sit near a changed threshold.

**Keeps the licence records.** For each shipment under an open licence, it fills the register from the order, the delivery note and the rating, and flags missing end-user details before the goods leave.

The hours are only half of it. A product rated wrongly can ship without a licence it needed, which is an offence under the Customs and Excise Management Act and the Export Control Order. HMRC offers compound settlements only where the breach was inadvertent or due to weak internal controls and the exporter told HMRC about it, and since 2026 [it publishes the names](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/businesses-agreeing-to-a-compound-settlement-for-strategic-export-and-sanction-offences/agreed-compound-settlements-for-strategic-export-and-sanction-offences). A rating file that shows what was checked, by whom and against which list version is what turns a mistake into a weakness in controls rather than something worse.

## Where it goes wrong

<Callout title="The assistant proposes. A named person rates." tone="warning">
A control list rating is a legal judgement about your goods. The model shows the entries, the thresholds and your figures side by side. Your export control manager decides, signs, and answers for it. When in doubt, ask a specialist adviser, especially while ECJU's classification service is paused.
</Callout>

**The list text is the authority, not the model's memory.** The assistant must work from the current consolidated list you load, quote the entry text it compared, and record the version. A model that "knows" the list from training will be out of date by 1 December.

**Catch-all controls are not on any list.** A product that is not listed can still need a licence if you know or suspect a military or weapons of mass destruction end use. That is a check on the customer and the order, not the product, and it belongs in your order screening, with a person deciding.

**Technical data is sensitive.** Drawings and source code are often the controlled technology themselves. Run the assistant where your company's security rules allow, never by pasting controlled technical data into a public chatbot, where the upload itself may count as a transfer of controlled technology.

## Tools that fit

Most exporters keep ratings in a column of the ERP item master and the evidence in someone's folder. Start there: an assistant that writes a rating record per item, links the evidence, and fills the ERP field only after a person signs. Global trade management systems can hold the ratings and screen orders, but they still need someone to produce the rating in the first place.

## Questions exporters ask about AI and export controls

### Can I still get a control list classification from ECJU?

Not for new requests at the moment. Notice to Exporters 2026/22 paused the Control List Classification service from 12 October 2026. Requests received before then are still answered, and ECJU said it would give an update before the end of 2026. Exporters remain responsible for deciding whether their goods are controlled.

### When do the new UK control lists apply?

The Export Control (Amendment) Regulations 2026 (SI 2026/1150) were laid before Parliament on 3 November 2026 and are due to come into force on 1 December 2026, subject to parliamentary procedure. They update the military list in Schedule 2 of the Export Control Order 2008 and Annex I of the assimilated Dual-Use Regulation.

### Can AI decide whether my product needs an export licence?

It should not. AI can compare your product's figures with each candidate entry and show where a threshold is met, missed or unknown, but the rating is a decision the exporter is responsible for and a named person should make it.

### What records do I need when I export under an open general licence?

Article 29 of the Export Control Order 2008 requires registers that identify the goods, the dates, the quantities, your name and address, the consignee and, so far as known, the end user, plus anything the licence itself requires. They must be kept for at least three years from the end of the calendar year of the export, four for some licences, or longer if the licence says so.

## Rate from evidence, not memory

Every product you export needs a rating someone can defend: the entries considered, the figures compared, the list version, a name and a date. Let AI read the datasheets and do the comparison, and keep the decision with the person who signs.
