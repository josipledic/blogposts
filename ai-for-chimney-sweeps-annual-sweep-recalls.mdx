AI can help a chimney sweep fill the diary before the first cold week instead of during it. Every sweep already leaves a record behind: the address, the appliance, the fuel, the date, what came out of the flue and anything that needs fixing. Plain rules turn those records into a list of who is due and when. A language model then writes each customer a short message with their own details, a few real slots and your price, sorts the replies, and drafts the sweep certificate from the notes you dictate on the job. You still sweep, inspect and decide whether an appliance is safe to use.

The demand side is easy to read in the official figures.

Fewer chimney fires is good news, and regular sweeping is part of why. It also tells you something about the work: it is seasonal, it is preventive, and the customer only thinks about it when the evenings get cold. That is when every sweep in the area gets the same phone call on the same weekend.

## How often customers should book

There is no single interval. [Hertfordshire Fire and Rescue Service](https://www.hertfordshire.gov.uk/services/fire-and-rescue/home-fire-safety/chimney-fire-safety.aspx), passing on the HETAS recommendation, says to sweep "at least twice a year when burning wood or bituminous house coal" and "at least once a year when burning smokeless fuels." The same page tells people to call a professional if they see soot falling back, tar, or smoke coming back into the room.

So the right recall date depends on the fuel, how much the appliance is used and what you found last time. You know all three. Your customer usually doesn't, and a generic "time for your annual sweep" postcard doesn't tell them.

## Where it breaks today

- **The diary jams in October.** Customers who used the stove all winter book when it gets cold again. Summer, when a sweep is easiest to fit in, stays quiet.
- **The record stays in the van.** Fuel, appliance, condition of the liner and the amount of soot are on a paper sheet or in the notes app. Nothing reads them again until next year.
- **Certificates take evenings.** Customers want a certificate for their landlord, their insurer or their own records. Writing them up after a full day is the job that slips.
- **Replies come from everywhere.** "Can you do a Saturday?", "how much for two flues?", "we've had the stove taken out" and "we moved" arrive by text, email, voicemail and Facebook.
- **Follow-up work is forgotten.** A cracked pot, a missing cowl or a bird guard you recommended gets mentioned once and then lost.

## Where AI fits

**A due list from your own records.** Each week the system lists customers whose next sweep falls in the coming weeks, based on the last visit and the interval you set per fuel: twice a year for wood and house coal, once for smokeless fuel, or whatever you recommend after looking at the flue. This is plain logic on your data, not a language model, and you can check every line.

**A message with their details in it.** For each customer the model drafts a short message from last year's record: "We swept your wood burner on 14 March. Because you burn logs, I'd book the next sweep before the heating season. I have Tuesday or Thursday morning next week, £X as last time." The price and the slots come from your price list and your calendar, never from the model.

**Spreading the season.** Customers who burn all winter get offered summer and early autumn slots first. Once those are booked, the late October weekend has room for the people who really did forget.

**Replies sorted and answered.** Each reply is tagged: wants to book, wants a price for more flues, appliance removed, moved house, opted out, or "a person should read this". Anything that mentions smoke in the room, a smell of fumes, a recent chimney fire or a CO alarm goes straight to you, not into a queue.

**Certificates from your notes.** You dictate a voice note on the job: appliance, fuel, method, what came out, condition, recommendations. The model fills in your certificate template from it, you check the draft on your phone, and the customer gets it before you leave the street. Recommended repairs go on a follow-up list with a quote attached.

The hours matter, but the bigger effect is the shape of the year. Every sweep you move from October to June is a slot free for a paying customer when demand peaks. Measure it: the share of your sweeps booked before September, before and after.

## Where it goes wrong

<Callout title="The model never judges a chimney" tone="warning">
Whether a flue is safe to use is your call, made on site. The model only repeats what you recorded. It never tells a customer that a stove is "fine to keep using", and it never writes a certificate you haven't read. Any reply about smoke, fumes, a CO alarm or a chimney fire goes to a person the same day, with the advice to stop using the appliance until it has been checked.
</Callout>

**Marketing rules.** A reminder about a service you carried out is close to a service message, but a booking offer is still marketing. The [ICO's guidance on electronic mail marketing](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guide-to-pecr/electronic-and-telephone-marketing/electronic-mail-marketing/) allows the "soft opt-in" only for your own customers who were given a simple way to opt out when you collected their details and in every message after. Record which channel each customer agreed to.

**Certificates are your signature.** A certificate says what you did and found. The draft must only contain what is in your notes. If the model adds a finding, a measurement or a standard you didn't mention, the draft goes back.

**Old records.** A customer you swept three years ago may have replaced the stove or moved. Show the date of the last visit in every message and let the reply update the record.

## Tools that fit

Start with whatever you use now. Several booking and certificate apps for sweeps already send reminders and email certificates. If yours does both well, the missing pieces are usually the recall interval set per fuel, the message written from your own notes and a reply inbox that sorts itself. A custom layer pays off when your records are on paper or in free text, when replies come in on three channels, or when you run more than one van.

## Questions sweeps ask

### Won't customers find reminders pushy?

Less than with a generic flyer. A message that names their stove, the date you were there and the fuel they burn is useful. Offer two or three slots and make it easy to say no.

### Can AI tell from a photo whether a flue needs work?

Not in a way you should rely on. Camera inspections are a separate tool for you to use and interpret. The workflow here only uses what you recorded.

### Is this worth it for a one-person business?

Usually yes, because the one person is also the office. Start with a due list from last year's jobs, sent by hand. Add drafted messages and certificate drafts once you see how many customers it finds.

## A rule for every sweep

Every sweep should end with a certificate in the customer's inbox and a next date in your system. Let AI write the certificate drafts, the recall messages and the reply sorting, and keep every judgement about a chimney with you.
