Picture the first Monday after the first frost. A small tyre shop with two bays and about 600 sets of wheels in its tyre hotel opens at 7:30, and the phone is already ringing. By ten o'clock there have been forty calls, nearly all asking the same thing: "Can you fit my winter tyres today?" Two customers are at the counter without an appointment, one of them in on summer tyres through slush.

None of this is a surprise. The shop knew in September that this Monday was coming, and it knew exactly who would be calling, because each of those 600 sets sits on a rack with a name, a plate number and a date on its tag. The changeover rush is the most predictable event in the tyre business, and most shops still meet it with a ringing phone and a paper diary.

This is a 30-day plan for using the records you already have to move demand out of the peak, and an assistant to answer the calls that still come.

Read the second and third numbers together. More than half of owners hand the job to a professional, and more than half of those leave their wheels behind. That is your customer list, and it's already in your system. The peak is thinning slowly, as all-season tyres gain ground at the expense of summer and winter tyres, according to the BRV, the German tyre trade association. For a shop with a tyre hotel, the rush hasn't gone anywhere.

What triggers the phone isn't the calendar, it's the weather. Germany's rule of thumb is "O bis O", October to Easter, but [the ADAC itself says](https://presse.adac.de/meldungen/adac-ev/technik/winterreifenpflicht.html) it only applies conditionally, and its regional clubs put [the weather ahead of the date](https://presse.adac.de/regionalclubs/nordrhein-westfalen/adac-nordrhein-zum-reifenwechsel-wetterlage-wichtiger-als-o-bis-o-regel.html), with temperatures regularly below about 7 degrees as the signal. In practice the phones start when frost or snow is forecast.

There is a legal edge too, specific to Germany. Since 1 October 2024, a tyre only counts as a winter tyre under the situational winter tyre rule (§ 2 Abs. 3a StVO) if it carries the Alpine symbol, the three-peak mountain with a snowflake. Tyres marked only M+S no longer qualify, and driving in wintry conditions without suitable tyres costs at least 60 euros and a point, [according to the ADAC](https://www.adac.de/news/auto-winterreifen-neue-regel-oktober/) and [autohaus.de](https://www.autohaus.de/nachrichten/schadenbusiness/winterreifen-das-alpine-symbol-ist-ab-morgen-pflicht-3559605). Two winters on, some of your stored sets are probably still M+S only. Those customers have a real reason to hear from you before the first snow.

## What the rush costs you now

Some rough arithmetic, with assumptions rather than measurements. Say a shop has 600 stored sets and 250 other customers in the season. If each booked job takes 1.5 calls at 2.5 minutes, that's 850 × 1.5 × 2.5 = about 3,200 minutes, or 53 hours of phone time, most of it in three weeks.

The bays are the harder limit. If a changeover with balancing takes 45 minutes of bay time, two bays working eight hours do roughly 21 cars a day, so 600 stored sets are about 28 working days of work. You can't absorb that in the ten days after the first frost. Somebody waits, somebody goes elsewhere, and the person on the phone is the person who should be at the lift. The goal isn't to answer calls faster. It's to need fewer of them.

## Start with the list you already own

A tyre hotel is a customer database with wheels attached. Storage systems such as [etirehotel](https://www.efleetcon.com/reifeneinlagerungssoftware), RLV, RESY, SoftWheels or [AJE's software](https://aje.de/reifeneinlagerungs-software/) hold most of what you need, and some send changeover reminders themselves. Those are names I found offering storage management in Germany, not products I've tested.

If your records are a spreadsheet or a binder, that's where AI pays for itself first. The same size gets typed five ways ("205/55R16", "205 55 16", "205/55 R 16 91H"), and the notes are free text ("Conti winter, front 5mm, rear ok"). A model can turn those into clean fields in an afternoon. You check a sample of fifty rows before trusting it with the rest.

## Which sets deserve a second look

Once the data is clean, you can flag the sets where an early conversation is worth having. Two numbers matter most: the legal minimum and the level where the ADAC recommends replacing tyres.

<Bars title="Tread depth: the law versus the recommendation" unit=" mm" caption="The legal minimum is a floor, not a target. The ADAC's own test of worn tyres found losses in aquaplaning and handling already measurable at 4 mm." source="ADAC" href="https://www.adac.de/rund-ums-fahrzeug/ausstattung-technik-zubehoer/reifen/sicherheit/reifenprofil/" data={[

  { label: "ADAC: replace summer tyres at", value: 3, display: "3 mm" },
  { label: "Legal minimum in Germany", value: 1.6, display: "1.6 mm" }

Below 1.6 mm the tyre is illegal. In Germany that costs [60 euros and a point](https://www.juraforum.de/lexikon/abgefahrene-reifen-bussgeld); in the UK, where the minimum is also 1.6 mm, it can be [up to £2,500 and 3 penalty points per tyre](https://www.gov.uk/check-vehicle-safe). The space between the floor and the recommendation holds most of the safety margin, and most of the commercial opportunity. The ADAC found [measurable losses at 4 mm](https://www.adac.de/rund-ums-fahrzeug/ausstattung-technik-zubehoer/reifen/sicherheit/abgefahrene-reifen/) and a much steeper decline between 4 and 2 mm.

| Flag | Rule | What happens next |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Winter set without the Alpine symbol | Marked M+S only | The invitation explains it no longer counts as a winter tyre in Germany |
| Set at or under the ADAC level | 4 mm winter or all-season, 3 mm summer, last recorded | The fitter measures on the day and quotes only if it's confirmed |
| Set at or near 1.6 mm | Legal minimum | Priority slot, new tyres quoted in advance |
| Set older than your own age limit | DOT code | The invitation says the fitter will check |
| No measurement for over a year | Record is stale | No claim in the message, fitter measures on the day |

None of that needs AI. It's a filter on a spreadsheet. What AI adds is the work around it: cleaning the records, writing each invitation for the right set, and handling replies. The decision point stays with a person. The fitter measures at the bay, the customer decides what to buy, and no message tells someone their tyres are unsafe based on a record that might be six months old. "Your winter set was at 4.5 mm in April, so we'll check it when we swap them" is true. "Your tyres are dangerous" isn't something a database can know.

A fitter also spots worn brake pads or a split sidewall at changeover. [Intake for independent garages](/posts/ai-for-auto-repair-diagnostics-intake) covers what to do with that.

## The 30-day plan

Count back from the day you expect frost to send customers to the phone: late September for the winter changeover, early March for the summer one. If your phone is already ringing, start at day 11.

<Phase when="Days 1 to 3" title="Pull and clean the list">Export the records, tidy sizes and notes, add the flags from the table. Check fifty rows by hand.</Phase>
<Phase when="Days 4 to 7" title="Set rules and lanes">Decide slots per day, how long a stored-set swap takes against new tyres plus balancing, and which hours stay open for walk-ins.</Phase>
<Phase when="Days 8 to 10" title="Open booking, test the phone">Put the booking page live, route busy-line calls to the assistant, and make awkward test calls with your own staff.</Phase>
<Phase when="Days 11 to 14" title="Invite the first wave">Customers whose wheels have waited longest, plus flagged sets, with quiet weekday slots up front.</Phase>
<Phase when="Days 15 to 24" title="Waves two and three">Adjust the offer from what wave one filled. Text a reminder the evening before each slot, and offer cancelled slots to a waitlist.</Phase>
<Phase when="Days 25 to 30" title="The peak">A person at the counter, the assistant on the phone, and ten minutes every morning on the callback list.</Phase>
</Timeline>

Send invitations in three waves of similar size rather than to everyone at once. A message that reaches 600 people together recreates the rush in your inbox. It's the same idea as in [the bike shop spring rush](/posts/ai-for-bike-shops-seasonal-service): move predictable work out of the peak.

The invitation works best when it's specific:

> Hi Ms Weber, your winter set (Continental, 205/55 R16) has been with us since April. Changeover slots for the new season are open: weekdays after 3 pm are free until mid-October, and Saturdays are filling fast. We'll measure the tread when we swap them and tell you before doing anything else. Book here, or reply with a day that suits you.

The model writes that for every customer. You read a sample before anything goes out.

## When the phone still rings

Some customers will never open a booking link. They call. The assistant's job is narrow: identify the set, offer open slots, confirm, and hand anything unusual to a person.

<Step title="Call arrives" who="Phone system" time="08:15">In the peak, calls go to the assistant when lines are busy or the shop is closed. Otherwise nothing changes.</Step>
<Step title="Greet and disclose" who="AI" tone="ai" time="0:00">It says it's the shop's automated assistant and can book a changeover or pass the call to a person.</Step>
<Step title="Find the customer and set" who="AI" tone="ai" time="30 sec">It asks for the plate number and confirms the stored set: "Winter set, 205/55 R16, stored since April."</Step>
<Step title="Offer open slots" who="AI" tone="ai" time="1 min">The next free slots from the booking calendar, and only those. Quiet days first.</Step>
<Step title="Confirm by text" who="System">An SMS with date, time and address, and a reminder the evening before.</Step>

Three rules keep this safe. The assistant never says a tyre is safe or unsafe, it quotes only prices you've published, and it never squeezes anyone into a slot the calendar doesn't show.

## When a booking page is enough

Online booking alone handles most of this. A booking page with SMS reminders, linked in the invitation, on your Google profile and in your busy-line message, takes most customers off the phone. The assistant is for the ones who prefer to call. I'd add it when the phone log shows unanswered calls in the peak, not before.

As for cost, these are my rough planning assumptions, not quotes. A booking tool with SMS reminders is a monthly fee in the tens of euros. An AI phone assistant is usually billed by usage, so the cost follows the peak; ask for the price per minute and a monthly cap. Setup is mostly your own time, a few days for the list, the rules and the test calls. Connecting to your tyre hotel software can take longer, so ask for a demo with your own data.

## Data protection and contacting customers

A name plus a licence plate is personal data, and a few things follow from that.

**Email invitations.** In Germany, advertising by email normally needs consent. [An exception in § 7 Abs. 3 UWG](https://www.it-recht-kanzlei.de/e-mail-werbung-ohne-einwilligung-moeglich.html) lets you promote your own similar services to existing customers if you got the address in connection with a sale, they haven't objected, and you told them about the right to object when you collected the address and in every message. Whether a plain "your wheels are waiting" counts as advertising is a grey area. I'd treat it as advertising and add the opt-out line. Rules elsewhere differ, so check yours.

**The vendor.** A tool that handles customer data needs a data processing agreement under Article 28 GDPR. Don't paste your customer list into a general chatbot.

**The phone.** If you record calls, say so and get consent; in Germany, recording without it is a criminal offence under § 201 StGB. Since August 2026, callers also have to be told they're talking to an AI under [Article 50 of the EU AI Act](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/transparency-obligations-under-article-50-ai-act). One short greeting covers both.

## Questions tyre shop owners ask

### Is it OK to email the customers in my tyre hotel?

In Germany, often yes under the existing-customer rule in § 7 Abs. 3 UWG, if you meet all its conditions, including the opt-out notice. A plain service reminder is less clear-cut than a promotion, so ask your data protection contact, and collect email consent properly when a new set goes into storage.

### How reliable is a tread depth flag from old records?

It's a prompt, not a measurement. The record may be from last spring. Use it to decide who gets an early invitation, and measure on the day before recommending anything.

### Will all-season tyres end the changeover rush?

Not soon. All-season tyres are still gaining share, according to the BRV, and the DAT survey above has about one owner in five already on them. But more than half of owners still use a workshop, and most of those store their wheels there.

## Where I'd start

Invite before they call. Pull the list this week, flag the sets you'd want to look at anyway, and send the first wave to the hundred customers whose wheels have waited longest, with a few quiet slots at the top. The booking page does most of the work. Add the assistant when the phone log says it's needed.
