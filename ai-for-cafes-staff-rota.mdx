Picture a 40-seat café with a small terrace. The owner writes next week's rota on Sunday night, copying last week's spreadsheet and nudging a few names. Last Saturday was grey and drizzly, and two people were plenty. This Saturday will be 24 degrees and dry, the farmers' market is on, and she has two people again. By 11:00 the queue reaches the door, one person is stuck at the till, the other runs between terrace and counter, and the part-timer due at 13:00 gets a panicked call.

None of that was bad luck. A rota is a forecast with rules attached: how many guests will arrive, hour by hour, and who may legally and sensibly serve them. Both halves can be improved with data most cafés already hold.

I'll use Germany as the worked example, because its rules are specific, strict and public. The structure carries over elsewhere.

## Why the rota matters so much

In a member survey at the start of 2026, the hospitality association DEHOGA found that [75.2% of businesses named personnel costs their biggest burden](https://www.tageskarte.io/zahlen/detail/gastgewerbe-verzeichnet-verhaltenen-jahresauftakt-2026.html), ahead of bureaucracy (67%). The sector also leans on Minijobs, German part-time jobs with an earnings cap. According to Federal Employment Agency figures reported by [Tageskarte](https://www.tageskarte.io/zahlen/detail/arbeitsmarktdaten-belegen-hohe-bedeutung-der-minijobs-im-gastgewerbe.html), hospitality Minijobs were 7.6% above their 2019 level in April 2026.

A rota with one person too many on slow days leaks money. One a person short on peak days loses sales and wears the team out. One that breaks a rule creates a risk no sales figure pays back.

## Put the rules in a table before anything else

The model's job is to work inside your rules, not to invent them. Write them down once, as data. For a German café, the list I'd start with looks like this.

| Rule | What it says | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Minijob cap, 2026 | 603 € a month, 7,236 € a year, about 43 hours at the 13.90 € minimum wage. From January 2027, 14.60 € minimum wage and an expected cap of 633 €, again about 43 hours | [Deutsche Rentenversicherung](https://www.deutsche-rentenversicherung.de/DRV/DE/Ueber-uns-und-Presse/Presse/Meldungen/2026/260223-minijob-midijob-verdienstgrenzen-steigen.html), [Bundesregierung](https://www.bundesregierung.de/breg-de/aktuelles/mindestlohn-steigt-2391010), [verdi](https://www.verdi.de/arbeit-recht/minijob-2026-verdienstgrenze-603-euro-alle-regeln) |
| Unforeseen extra shifts | In up to two months within 12, a Minijobber may earn up to 1,206 €, for example covering sickness. Seasonal extra work is predictable and does not qualify | [Minijob-Zentrale](https://magazin.minijob-zentrale.de/minijob-verdienstgrenze/) |
| Hours and breaks | 8 hours a day, up to 10 if the average stays at 8 over six months. Breaks fixed in advance: 30 minutes above 6 hours, 45 above 9 | [§ 3 ArbZG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/arbzg/__3.html), [§ 4 ArbZG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/arbzg/__4.html) |
| Rest between shifts | 11 hours. Venues serving guests may cut it by one hour if each cut is made up to 12 hours within a month | [§ 5 ArbZG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/arbzg/__5.html) |
| Sundays | Allowed in hospitality, but 15 Sundays a year must stay free for each employee | [§ 10 ArbZG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/arbzg/__10.html), [§ 11 ArbZG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/arbzg/__11.html) |
| Under 18 | 8 hours a day, 40 a week, 12 hours rest, five-day week, 60 minutes of breaks above 6 hours. Only between 6:00 and 20:00, or until 22:00 for over-16s in hospitality. Sundays allowed, but two a month stay free | [§ 8](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/jarbschg/__8.html), [§ 11](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/jarbschg/__11.html), [§ 13](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/jarbschg/__13.html), [§ 14](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/jarbschg/__14.html), [§ 15](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/jarbschg/__15.html), [§ 17 JArbSchG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/jarbschg/__17.html) |
| On-call work | Weekly hours must be in the contract; if not, 20 a week are deemed agreed. Four days' notice | [§ 12 TzBfG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/tzbfg/__12.html) |
| Time records | Start, end and length of each day, for Minijobbers and in hospitality, within seven days | [§ 17 MiLoG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/milog/__17.html) |

Two of these are easy to break without noticing. The first is the **Minijob running total**. With fluctuating pay, the annual figure matters, not each month: the employer estimates the next twelve months at the start and has to stay inside 7,236 €. A rota that hands someone every extra Saturday in summer can break that in October, unnoticed until payroll. The second is **contracts without hours**: "as needed" with no weekly figure means 20 hours a week in the eyes of the law, roughly 1,200 € a month at minimum wage, twice the Minijob cap.

Keep the limits editable, because Minijobs are being reworked. The pension commission proposed in June 2026 to end their special status, which has not become law. What is moving instead is higher employer charges: the flat health contribution is due to rise from 13% to an expected 17.5% in January 2027, and the cabinet proposes a flat tax of 5% instead of 2% from then. The [Minijob-Zentrale keeps a running chronology](https://magazin.minijob-zentrale.de/aktuelle-minijob-vorhaben/) (1 October 2026).

## From till to forecast

Your till already knows more about next Saturday than you do. Export sales and order counts by hour and weekday for the last 12 to 24 months. Most systems export sales as CSV, and [orderbird documents how](https://support.orderbird.com/de_DE/berichte-auf-my-orderbird/myorderbird-umsatzuerbersicht). First check whether your export has a timestamp per order. The shape of the day matters more than the total. This is an invented Saturday.

If one person at the counter handles about 20 orders an hour (an assumption you should measure), 10:00 needs two at the counter plus someone on the floor, while 17:00 needs one. That is the difference between staffing by shift and staffing by hour.

Then add what explains the rest. Weather first: the German weather service DWD publishes forecasts and observations as [open data](https://www.dwd.de/DE/leistungen/opendata/faqs_opendata.html), without registration and under a CC BY 4.0 licence. The free [Bright Sky API](https://brightsky.dev/) serves part of it as simple JSON, though it is a hobby-run service, so keep a fallback. Then your calendar: school holidays, public holidays (they differ by federal state), markets, street festivals.

How big the weather effect is depends on your terrace and your street. I couldn't find an independent German figure worth quoting, so measure it: compare the same weekday and hour on dry and rainy days in your own data.

## Where AI helps, and where it doesn't

Most of this is not AI. A pivot table gives you a weekday-by-hour average. Rota tools such as Papershift, Planday or Shiftbase handle availability, swaps and time recording, and some flag rule conflicts. If yours checks the three things that matter (11 hours rest, Minijob running total, under-18 limits), you may need no model at all. Test the Minijob limit first, since not every tool tracks it. A five-person café with a fixed weekly pattern needs a template, not a project.

Where a model earns its place:

- **Turning mess into structure.** "Tuesday and Thursday mornings, nothing before 9 on Wednesday" in a WhatsApp message becomes an availability record. A year of till exports becomes a clean table.
- **Explaining the forecast.** "Saturday looks like 1.3 times a normal one: it's dry, above 22 degrees, and the last three such days were up 22% to 35%." You can argue with that sentence, unlike a bare number.
- **Drafting options.** Two or three drafts with the trade-offs in plain language, and flags for anything touching your rule table.

Keep the arithmetic out of the model. A script or your rota tool computes hours, rest gaps and euro totals, and the model reads the results. A language model that miscounts 22:30 to 08:00 does so with total confidence. It's the conflict-checking pattern from [home care scheduling](/posts/ai-for-home-care-scheduling), with lower stakes. And the model can't see the wedding party next door, which is why the owner signs off.

## What a draft looks like

This is the output I'd want on Sunday evening. Every number is invented.

```text
Saturday. Forecast 24 C, dry. Market day.
Expected demand: about 1.3 x a normal Saturday
(last 3 comparable days: +28%, +35%, +22%).

Proposed
08:00 to 14:00  Tom (open), Jonas (terrace from 10:00)
10:00 to 17:00  Aylin
12:00 to 20:00  Lena (17), 60 min break
14:30 to 16:30  open: extra counter for the 15:00 peak

FLAGS
1. Tom closes Fri 22:30 and opens Sat 08:00: 9.5 h rest.
   Not allowed, even with the one-hour hospitality exception.
   Options: swap with Jonas, or start Tom at 09:30.
2. Aylin (Minijob): 3,180 EUR so far this year, 7,540 EUR
   projected against the 7,236 EUR limit. Decide: cut about
   22 hours over the summer, or discuss a new contract.
OK: breaks fixed, Lena inside her youth limits, all other
rest gaps 11 h or more.
```

The flags decide nothing. They put the two real choices in front of a person, with options worked out. That's the human decision point: the owner picks, changes or rejects, and only then does anything go to the team.

The last item is practice, not law: outside on-call work I know of no fixed statutory notice period for a rota, but people resent one that changes the week before. Ordering people in at short notice is on-call work, with the four-day rule and the Minijob limit attached.

## Law and data you need to know

**The AI Act.** Annex III of the EU AI Act lists employment systems that [allocate tasks based on individual behaviour or personal traits, or monitor and evaluate performance](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/annex/3/) as high-risk. The Digital Omnibus moves the high-risk obligations from 2 August 2026 to 2 December 2027 and, according to the law firm [Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/07/EU-AI-Act-Update-Digital-Omnibus-Finalizes-8-Compliance-Changes), is in force (checked September 2026). I'm not a lawyer, but my practical reading is to keep the assistant to availability, demand and your rule table, with no performance rankings or sick-day histories.

**Time recording.** In 2022 the Federal Labour Court ruled that employers must [record the start and end of daily working time](https://www.bundesarbeitsgericht.de/entscheidung/1-abr-22-21/). A ministry draft of June 2026 would make electronic recording the rule, with five years' transition for employers with 11 to 49 staff and no electronic requirement for those with up to ten, according to [Handwerksblatt](https://www.handwerksblatt.de/themen-specials/arbeitszeit-erfassen-aber-wie/elektronische-erfassung-der-arbeitszeit-wird-pflicht) (1 October 2026). It is still being coordinated between ministries, so don't buy a time clock yet.

**Staff data.** Names, availability and contract hours are personal data, so you need a processing agreement (Art. 28 GDPR) with any provider that sees them, and an employee privacy notice that says the rota is drafted with software and that you decide. Sickness is health data under Art. 9: give the model initials and availability, never reasons for absence. If you have a works council, it co-determines start, end and distribution of daily working hours under [§ 87 BetrVG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/betrvg/__87.html).

**Outside Germany.** In the UK, workers get [11 hours rest between working days](https://www.gov.uk/rest-breaks-work), and the National Living Wage is [£12.71 from April 2026](https://www.gov.uk/national-minimum-wage-rates). As far as I found, US federal law has no schedule-notice rule. Oregon requires 14 days' written notice but [only from employers with 500 or more employees](https://www.oregon.gov/boli/workers/pages/predictive-scheduling.aspx), and some cities have their own laws.

## Questions café owners ask

### Can AI make my staff schedule automatically?

It can draft one, and a good draft saves an hour on Sunday night. It can't publish one: the rules sit in a table, a script does the arithmetic, and you decide what goes to the team.

### Is it legal to use AI for staff scheduling in Germany?

Nothing forbids using software to draft a rota. What counts is the result: it must respect the working time rules, the youth rules and your contracts, and you stay responsible as the employer. Data protection and any works council apply too. The AI Act's high-risk list only bites if the system allocates work by behaviour or traits, or judges performance.

### How much sales history do I need?

Eight to twelve weeks show the weekday-by-hour shape. A full year shows seasons and holidays. A new café starts with a template and learns as it goes.

## Where I'd start

Write the rule table and export sales by hour before looking at any tool. If your rota already satisfies the rules and matches demand within an hour of staffing, stop there. If you still count Minijob euros by hand on Sunday night, or staff Saturday as if it were Tuesday, the 30 days are worth it.
