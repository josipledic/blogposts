AI can take the reading out of public tendering for a construction contractor: every new notice checked against what you actually build and where, the tender pack read the day it lands for deadlines, mandatory requirements, risky clauses and questions worth asking, and first drafts of the quality answers built from your own past bids and project records. Your estimator still prices the job and a director still decides whether to bid. What changes is that those decisions start on day two of the tender period instead of day fifteen.

Public work is a large, steady market for contractors who can bid well, and the rules that govern it changed in February 2025.

## What the Procurement Act changed for bidders

[The Procurement Act 2023](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/54/contents) applies to procurements started on or after 24 February 2025. For a contractor the parts that shape the bid desk are:

- **Fixed minimum periods.** A tendering period of at least 25 days when tenders go in electronically and the notice and documents are published together, 30 days when they are not all published at once, and 10 days after a qualifying planned procurement notice or in genuine urgency ([section 54](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/54/section/54)). The authority must also allow for site visits and subcontracting when it sets the period.
- **Assessment summaries for everyone.** Before a contract award notice, the authority must send each assessed bidder a summary of how its tender was scored and, if different, how the winning tender was scored ([section 50](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/54/section/50)).
- **An 8 working day standstill.** The contract cannot be signed until eight working days after the contract award notice is published ([section 51](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/54/section/51)). That is the window in which a losing bidder reads the summary and decides whether to challenge.

The 25 days sounds generous until you count what has to happen in it: download and read the pack, walk the site, get subcontractor and supplier prices, ask clarification questions before the authority's cut-off, write the quality answers, price, review and upload.

## Where it breaks today

- **Notices are found late.** Alerts arrive in one person's inbox, mixed with irrelevant lots, frameworks and supply contracts. A good fit is spotted on day eight.
- **Bid/no-bid is a gut call.** Nobody has time to read 300 pages before deciding, so the decision is made on the title and the value, and the risky clause is found after you have spent a week on it.
- **Questions are asked too late or not at all.** Contradictions between the specification, the drawings and the pricing schedule get priced around instead of raised, because the clarification deadline passed while the pack was still unread.
- **Every quality answer starts from a blank page.** The best answer you ever wrote on social value or site safety sits in a folder from 2023, and the person who wrote it has left.
- **Feedback is filed and forgotten.** Assessment summaries arrive, someone reads the score, and the next bid repeats the same weak section.

## Where AI fits

**Triage every notice.** The model reads each new tender notice and compares it with a short profile you write once: trades, contract sizes you can carry, regions, accreditations, insurance levels and the clients you have worked for. It sorts notices into "look now", "maybe" and "no", with one line on why. A person checks the "look now" list each morning.

**Read the whole pack on day one.** For a tender you are considering, it extracts every deadline (clarification cut-off, site visit, submission), every mandatory requirement and pass/fail question, the evaluation criteria and weightings, the contract form and any amendments to it, liquidated damages, retention, insurance levels and payment terms. It lists the conditions your company does not currently meet, so the bid/no-bid meeting is about facts.

**Find the questions worth asking.** It compares the specification, the drawings schedule and the pricing document and lists contradictions and gaps: an item specified but not in the bill, a drawing referenced but not issued, a programme that conflicts with an access restriction. Your estimator picks which ones to send. Asking before the cut-off is cheaper than pricing the risk.

**Draft quality answers from your own evidence.** From your past bids, case studies, method statements and project records, it drafts answers to each quality question within the word limit, and marks every claim with the source document it came from. Anything without a source is flagged for a person to fill or delete. It never invents a project, a number or an accreditation.

**Check compliance before upload.** Plain code, not the model, ticks every mandatory document and declaration against the list extracted on day one, so a missing form does not knock out a good bid.

**Learn from every result.** When an assessment summary arrives, it compares your scores with the winner's by criterion and adds the gaps to a short list your team reviews before the next bid.

## Where it goes wrong

<Callout title="The model does not price and does not sign" tone="warning">
Rates, risk allowances, programme, the decision to bid and every declaration you submit belong to named people in your business. The model reads, compares, flags and drafts. Build the workflow so nothing goes into a submission without a person approving it.
</Callout>

**Made-up evidence is the real risk.** A quality answer that claims a project you did not deliver, or a figure nobody measured, can get a bid excluded or a contract terminated later. Only let the model draft from your own documents, require a source for every claim, and have the person who knows the project check it.

**Tender documents are confidential.** Many tenders restrict who may see the documents. Use a model provider with a data processing agreement, no training on your data and storage in the UK or EU, and check the tender's confidentiality terms before you upload anything.

**Missed mandatory items stay missed.** Extraction is good but not perfect. Keep the compliance check as a list a person ticks against the original documents, not a promise from the model.

## Tools that fit

Tender alert services and bid management platforms already search notices, store answer libraries and track deadlines, and several now offer AI drafting. If you use one, the gaps are usually reading the full pack for contradictions, drafting from your own project records rather than a generic library, and turning assessment summaries into changes. A custom layer pays off when your evidence sits across a project system, a shared drive and old bid folders that no platform reads.

## Questions contractors ask about AI for tenders

### Can AI write a whole tender response?

It can draft most of the written quality answers from your past bids and project records, within the word limits, with a source for each claim. It should not set prices, programme or risk allowances, and a person who knows the work has to check every answer before it goes in.

### How much time is there to bid under the Procurement Act?

For a typical open tender with electronic submission and all documents issued with the notice, at least 25 days. It can be 10 days after a qualifying planned procurement notice or in genuine urgency, and 30 or 35 days when documents are issued later or tenders are not submitted electronically.

### What happens after I lose a tender?

Before the award notice, the authority sends you an assessment summary showing how your tender and the winning tender were scored. The contract cannot be signed for at least eight working days after the award notice. Read the summary quickly: it is your best evidence for the next bid and the only window for a challenge before signing.

## A rule for every tender

Every tender you consider should have its deadlines, mandatory requirements and risks extracted on day one, a bid/no-bid decision made by a named person within the first few days, questions sent before the cut-off, and its assessment summary reviewed when the result comes in. Let AI do the reading and the first drafts, and keep the pricing and the promises with your team.
